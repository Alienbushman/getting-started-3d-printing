# Reference Sites Teardown — getting-started-3d-printing

Surveyed 2026-05-16 for [getting-started-3d-printing:005].
Sign-off: alienbushman.

All sources are freely accessible (no paywall required for beginner content). Attribution policy noted per site.

---

| Site | URL | Does Well | Does Badly | UX Pattern to Steal | UX Pattern to Avoid |
|------|-----|-----------|------------|---------------------|---------------------|
| **Prusa Knowledge Base** | https://help.prusa3d.com | Structured troubleshooting trees, printer-specific step-by-step guides, high-quality embedded images, active maintenance. Content is CC-BY-SA 4.0 where noted. | Heavily Prusa-centric — settings, menus, and part names assume a Prusa machine. Less useful if you own anything else. | "What could go wrong" callout boxes embedded mid-step surface failure modes without derailing the happy path. | Deep model-specific navigation at the top obscures the beginner entry point; new visitors land mid-tree. |
| **All3DP** | https://all3dp.com | Broad topic coverage, good "best of" listicles, SEO-friendly URLs, quick-answer box at the top of each article. Commercial site; content is all-rights-reserved. | Ad density is high; some articles are thin listicles written for search rank rather than depth. Dates on articles are often misleading (evergreen content looks stale). | "Quick answer" summary box at the very top of the article — readers get the gist in 2 sentences before committing to the full read. | Injecting affiliate links and ad blocks mid-paragraph breaks reading flow and erodes trust on a reference site. |
| **Teaching Tech calibration hub** | https://teachingtechyt.com/calibration.html | Systematic, step-by-step calibration methodology with interactive calculators. Printer-agnostic. One of the most-linked beginner resources in the community. Content is shared openly. | Assumes the printer is already mechanically assembled and printing. Not a true zero-to-first-print resource. Video-first; the text-only path is incomplete. | Interactive parameter calculator embedded inline — users enter their values and get a result on the page without leaving. | Requiring a video watch before the text path makes sense; splitting the two into separate pages leaves text-only readers stranded. |
| **Printables.com** | https://www.printables.com | Tight integration of model card + guide content. Model cards surface license, print time, difficulty, and material at a glance. Strong filtering and sort. Content licensed per-model (mostly CC). | Some features (downloading certain files) require a free account. Prusa-branded; the platform curation leans toward Prusa hardware. | Model card information density: license, est. print time, difficulty, skill tag all visible without opening the model detail. | Surfacing sign-up prompts aggressively during model browsing creates friction before the user has seen enough value. |
| **Reddit r/3Dprinting wiki** | https://www.reddit.com/r/3Dprinting/wiki | Community-curated FAQ that reflects real beginner questions. Recommended hardware lists are reasonably up to date. Good "questions people actually ask" signal. | Reddit formatting constraints (no tables, limited images, link rot). Unsorted wall of links in some sections. No visual hierarchy. | FAQ structure derived from actual community questions — this is the ground truth for what beginners are actually confused about. | Unsorted link dumps without context or curation look authoritative but send readers on a dead-end hunt. |
| **Bambu Lab Wiki** | https://wiki.bambulab.com | Slick UI, fast page loads, good coverage of Bambu Studio slicer workflow. Pre-flight checklist concept is well-executed. All content is Bambu IP. | Entirely Bambu-ecosystem-specific. AMS multi-color workflow is prominent, which is not relevant to beginners on other hardware. Assumes their slicer. | "Before you print" pre-flight checklist framing — a short numbered list of checks before every print reduces failed prints without becoming a wall of prose. | Ecosystem lock-in framing in the intro ("with your Bambu printer") alienates readers who are researching before buying or who own other hardware. |
| **CNC Kitchen** | https://cnckitchen.com | Data-driven filament and print-settings comparisons with actual strength/stiffness measurements. Methodology is reproducible and cited. CC-style open sharing of test results. | Intermediate-to-advanced target audience; not a zero-to-first-print resource. Heavy technical vocabulary without inline glossary. | Comparison tables with actual measured numbers (tensile strength, impact resistance) rather than vague "this is stronger" claims. | Dense technical terminology on an otherwise well-structured page creates an intimidating first impression for beginners. |
| **Maker's Muse** | https://www.makersmuse.com | Practical "design for 3D printing" framing — real-world constraints turned into learnable rules. Good coverage of print orientation, wall thickness, and tolerance concepts. | Some content is older (2018–2021) and reflects hardware/software that has moved on. YouTube-first; article versions are often thin. | "Design for 3D printing" constraint-first framing: teach the rule (e.g. 45° overhang limit), then show what breaks when you violate it, then show the fix. | Paywalled premium design files mixed into free content; users can't tell what they can access without clicking through. |
